About the Palace

Nestled upon still waters that have mirrored centuries of sky, The Chandralok Palace is less a building than a held breath — a lake-born residence where Rajasthan’s royal lineage meets the quiet authority of the moon. Raised in sandstone and marble by those who understood that true power does not shout, the palace was conceived as a sanctuary for reflection: every courtyard opens toward the water, every corridor leads the eye toward light that softens at dusk.

Here, heritage is not displayed; it is lived. Carved jali screens filter the day into patterns that move like thought. Domes painted with celestial motifs recall an age when rulers measured wisdom by their capacity for stillness. The lake itself is the palace’s oldest teacher — its surface gathers the moon and returns it, doubled and calm, inviting the body to slow until breath and water share one rhythm.

Guests often speak of a subtle settling, as if the water and moonlight together ease the inner currents — the places where restlessness gathers. In the soft language of older wisdoms, such places are said to soothe the chakras: the cool clarity of the lake cooling the fire of overthinking, the silver of moonlight steadying the heart. Whether one arrives seeking splendour or silence, Chandralok offers both without spectacle — a private world where richness is measured in quiet, and history is felt in the cool of marble under an open hand.
